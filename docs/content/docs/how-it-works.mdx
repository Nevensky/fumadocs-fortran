What happens between the Fortran sources and the pages, so that a reader can predict the pages, and where they can
differ from what a compiler or FORD sees. The flags named here are on [Command line](/docs/reference/cli), and the
files the last step writes are on [Generated files](/docs/reference/generated-files).

## From sources to pages

`fumadocs-fortran generate` reads the sources with its own parser, in plain JavaScript: it runs no compiler, no
Python and nothing from the network. For each file it:

1. finds it: a file named on the command line is read whatever its extension, and a folder is searched for files
   with the extensions of Fortran, past folders whose name starts with a dot and `node_modules`;
2. reads it as UTF-8, or as Latin-1 when it is not valid UTF-8, and skips it with a warning when it is a fypp
   source, which needs fypp to run first;
3. decides its form, free or fixed, from the extension, or from the text for `.F` and `.fpp`, unless `--form` decides
   for every file;
4. runs its C preprocessor (cpp) when the file has a directive: conditions are evaluated with the macros given,
   `#include` reads the file's folder and the `-I` folders, and macros are expanded, though never inside strings or
   comments; then Fortran `include` lines are read in;
5. reads its statements, across continuation lines, with their comments and the directives of OpenMP, OpenACC,
   CUDA Fortran and the compilers' own;
6. parses them into the model: units, procedures, interfaces, types, variables and enums, with their arguments,
   attributes, uses, calls, directives, cpp conditions and doc comments, and a diagnostic for what it cannot read.

`fumadocs-fortran dump` prints this model as JSON. From it, `generate` renders each doc comment in its style (FORD,
Doxygen or LAPACK, see [Doc comments](/docs/reference/doc-comments)), resolves the names that link, looks library
names up in the registry, draws the graphs and writes the pages. Nothing is compiled or run, so the pages show what
the sources say, read by the rules below. CI checks the parser against public codes at pinned commits
(`scripts/fetch-corpus.sh` in the repository lists them), with gcc's preprocessor as the reference for cpp.

## One build at a time

A reference shows one build of the code: the one that the macros given with `-D` select. A condition that does not
hold switches its branch off, and nothing in that branch reaches the model or the pages. What a branch that is on
declares keeps its condition. Its badge is dashed and ends in "when USE_MPI", its row of a table says so, and so do
its item in a Calls line and its edge in a graph. `defined(USE_MPI)` reads as USE_MPI, `!defined(USE_MPI)` as not
USE_MPI, and the terms of a nested condition are joined by "and".

- [`-D NAME`](/docs/reference/cli#define) defines `NAME` as 1, and `-D NAME=VALUE` as the value, function-like
  macros such as `-D 'F(x)=x'` included.
- OpenMP's conditional lines, `!$` in free form and `c$`, `*$` or `!$` in columns 1 and 2 of fixed form, are code
  only when `_OPENMP` is defined, and comments otherwise, as a compiler without OpenMP reads them.
- [`--all-branches`](/docs/reference/cli#all-branches) reads every branch, so the pages show all of them, each with
  its condition, as `when USE_MPI` and `when not USE_MPI` side by side: no compiler builds that code at once. It also
  makes `!$` lines code.

The pages do not record the macros they were generated with: say on a page of your own which build the reference
shows. The Showcase, for one, is generated with `_OPENMP` and `USE_MPI` defined.

## Library names

The package ships a registry of the names of HPC libraries (`registry/`). From it, the generator gives a hover card
to the library routines of "Calls:" lines, and to `mpi_f08`'s types and `omp_lib`'s kinds in the tables, and colours
the library routines of the call graphs. It holds:

| Library | From | Entries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BLAS and LAPACK | Reference-LAPACK 3.12.1 | 2,265 routines, 155 of them BLAS |
| ScaLAPACK, with PBLAS, BLACS, its tools and REDIST | Reference-ScaLAPACK 2.2.2 | 1,028 routines |
| MPI | MPI 5.0, with 4.1, and Open MPI 5.0 | 463 procedures and callbacks callable from Fortran, and the 12 derived types of `mpi_f08` |
| OpenMP | the 6.1, 6.0 and 5.2 specifications | 469 directives, clauses, routines, environment variables, kinds and constants |
| OpenACC | the 3.4 specification | 147 directives, clauses, routines and environment variables |
| Fortran intrinsics | M_intrinsics | 218 procedures |

ScaLAPACK's count takes in the 18 routines that its SRC and TOOLS folders and PBLAS's PTOOLS write in C for Fortran
to call: `slamov`, `dlamov`, `clamov` and `zlamov`, `pcrot` and `pzrot`, `pslaiect`, `pdlaiectb` and `pdlaiectl`,
`pslasnbt` and `pdlasnbt`, `pslachkieee` and `pdlachkieee`, `reshape` and `sl_gridreshape`, and `PB_topget`,
`PB_topset` and `PB_freebuf`. The registry also holds the names of oneMKL's `lapack95` and `blas95`, OpenBLAS and
cuBLAS, which the pages do not use. `registry/SOURCES.md` lists the sources, each pinned by version and checksum.

The generator reads the registry the first time a page needs it. Without it, the names are code with no card, and
the graphs draw LAPACK's and ScaLAPACK's routines grey.

### Cards

- **Calls.** In the "Calls:" line of a procedure or a program, a library subroutine is a `FortranRef`. Its card has the
  routine's kind, name and library (`subroutine dgesv (LAPACK)`), the registry's summary of it, of at most 200
  characters, and a link to its documentation, each where the registry has one. The registry has a summary for every
  subroutine of BLAS, LAPACK, MPI and the intrinsics, for every subroutine of ScaLAPACK, PBLAS, BLACS, the tools and
  REDIST but `slaqr6` and `dlaqr6`, and for 23 of OpenMP's 68 subroutines and 14 of OpenACC's 53. A routine with no page
  to link to, such as the intrinsic `tokenize` or MPI's predefined callbacks, has a card without a link. A routine that
  a use statement renames is looked up by its own name. The line lists `CALL` statements, so a function such as `dnrm2`
  has no card there, though the call graphs draw it.
- **Types and kinds.** In a table, the derived types of `mpi_f08` (`type(MPI_Comm)`, `MPI_Datatype`, `MPI_Request`,
  `MPI_Status` and the others) and the kinds of `omp_lib` or `omp_lib_kinds` (`integer(omp_lock_kind)`) have a card
  when the declaration's scope uses the module. It may use it by `use` (`use, intrinsic :: omp_lib` too), an `only`
  list or a rename, through its host or through a module of the project that passes the name on. The card has the
  registry's line on the name, a link to the MPI 5.0 standard or the OpenMP specification, and the use statement it
  comes from. `use mpi`, whose handles are integers, gives none.
- **The project wins.** A procedure, generic interface or variable of the project, shown or not, is never taken for a
  library's, and neither is a dummy argument in scope or a procedure pointer the caller declares (in a `BLOCK`, in its
  specification part or in its host's). The project's own `dgemm` keeps its link, or stays code when it has no heading,
  and a call through such a pointer has no card. A module of the project named `mpi_f08`, such as a serial stub, gives
  its own types. An interface body that only describes a library routine, as code writes one for `dgesv`, does not
  count: a call links to it when it has a heading, and has the library's card when it has none. A `pointer` statement on
  its name makes it a procedure pointer, which does. A `CALL` of a name the registry has as a function, or of an
  intrinsic's name that another module's use statement gives, names some other subroutine and has no card. The exception
  is a name the registry also has as a subroutine: `call reshape` is ScaLAPACK's `reshape`, of its tools, not the
  intrinsic function.

### Where the cards link

Each library's cards link to the documentation that [`--links`](/docs/reference/cli#links), or `hpc.links`, chooses
for it, and Command line gives each library's choices and its default. A name its choice has no page for links as by
default, and the defaults have fallbacks of their own:

- an MPI procedure that Open MPI 5.0's manual lacks links to Open MPI's development manual, and the derived types of
  `mpi_f08` link to the MPI 5.0 standard, whichever MPI is chosen;
- a name that the OpenMP 5.2 specification has no section for links to the newest specification that has one;
- `random_init` and `split`, which fortran-lang.org lacks, link to gfortran's manual;
- BLACS, ScaLAPACK's tools and REDIST stay on Netlib with `mkl`, as oneMKL has no pages for them.

An unknown library or provider stops the run with a message. An MKL address is made from the routine's name and not
checked, as intel.com refuses scripts.
[`--library-cards none`](/docs/reference/cli#library-cards), or `hpc.cards: false`, leaves the names as code, with no
card, and keeps the graphs' colours.

### The registry's text on your site [#registry-text]

The summaries in the cards are quoted from other projects: those of BLAS and LAPACK from Reference-LAPACK, of
ScaLAPACK, PBLAS, its tools and REDIST from Reference-ScaLAPACK, of MPI's procedures from Open MPI's manual pages,
and of the intrinsics from M_intrinsics. The lines on OpenMP, OpenACC, BLACS, MPI's callbacks and types, `omp_lib`'s
kinds and a few ScaLAPACK routines written in C are the package's own. A site that publishes generated pages
publishes these summaries too. `registry/SOURCES.md` names each source with its version and licence, and
`registry/NOTICE` holds their copyright notices and licence texts; both ship in the package. These pages give no
legal advice. A line in the site's footer that links the notices is one way to credit them:

```tsx
<p>
  Library summaries from Reference-LAPACK, Reference-ScaLAPACK, Open MPI and M_intrinsics:{' '}
  <a href="https://github.com/Nevensky/fumadocs-fortran/blob/main/registry/NOTICE">notices</a>
</p>
```

## Graphs

- **What each graph draws.** A unit's dependencies are the modules that it and everything in it use, followed two
  steps deep through the project's modules, then what uses it, and its submodules or, for a submodule, its parents.
  A type's inheritance is the types it extends, up to the root, and every type that extends it. A procedure's calls
  are what it calls, two steps deep through the project's procedures, and what calls it. The overview has every
  module, submodule and program, with one node for each HPC library they use. A graph that would hold only its
  subject is left out.
- **Limits.** A graph draws at most 40 nodes, the last of them standing for the rest ("… 12 more"). The routines
  from outside the project go first, however many callers they have, the farthest first, then the farthest of the
  rest. A project of more than 80 units has no overview graph. From JavaScript, `graphs: { depth, maxNodes }` changes
  the two steps and the 40 nodes, and `maxNodes` the 80 units as well;
  [`--graphs none`](/docs/reference/cli#graphs) leaves every graph out.
- **Calls.** A call is a `CALL` statement or a function reference, resolved as Fortran resolves it: through `use`,
  renames, host association, generic interfaces and submodules. A type-bound one (`call m%solve(b)`,
  `n = m%order()`) is drawn when `m` is a dummy argument, a function result or a module variable, whose type the
  model knows, and not through a local variable, as the model keeps no local declarations. Intrinsic procedures are
  left out, and a function reference to a name the project does not declare is drawn only when the name is a known
  library's, such as LAPACK's `dlamch`, as it might as well be an array. A node bears the name as the source first
  writes it, so fixed-form code's `DLAMCH` stands beside its `DGEMM`. A `CALL` of a name that only an intrinsic
  function has names some other routine, drawn as one from outside the project; the registry knows ScaLAPACK's own
  `reshape`, which `call reshape` draws in ScaLAPACK's colour. A call through a procedure pointer the caller declares
  is not drawn.
- **Shapes.** A module and a type are boxes, a submodule a box with double sides, a program a stadium, a procedure a
  rounded box and a generic interface or binding a hexagon. A module or routine from outside the project is a rounded
  box, and a type from outside it a box. The graph's subject has a heavier border.
- **Colours.** Each kind of the project's entities has a colour: modules, submodules, programs (block data units with
  them), procedures (functions and subroutines alike), generics and types; an abstract type has a dashed border in
  the type's colour. A routine or module from outside the project takes its library's colour:
  - BLAS, LAPACK and ScaLAPACK: the routines the registry knows, BLAS first, so that the BLAS routines
    Reference-LAPACK ships stay BLAS, and PBLAS, BLACS, ScaLAPACK's tools and REDIST with ScaLAPACK;
  - MPI: `mpi_*` routines, and the modules `mpi`, `mpi_f08` and `mpif`;
  - OpenMP: `omp_*`, `omp_lib` and `omp_lib_kinds`; OpenACC: `acc_*` and `openacc`;
  - CUDA: routines whose names start with `cuda`, `cublas`, `cufft`, `curand`, `cusparse` or `cusolver`, in any case,
    and the modules `cudafor`, `cublas`, `cufft`, `curand`, `cusparse` and `cusolverDn`;
  - the coarray collectives (`co_sum`, `co_min`, `co_max`, `co_reduce`, `co_broadcast`).

  `graphs.classify`, a JavaScript option of `generate()` and `buildPages()`, takes the registry's place. It names the
  library of a routine the project does not define from its lower-case name, such as
  `(name) => (MYLIB.has(name) ? 'lapack' : null)` with a set of names. The command line has no such option. With it,
  or without the registry, the names alone still tell MPI, OpenMP, OpenACC, CUDA, the coarray collectives and BLAS.
  The other routines and modules from outside the project are grey and dashed, and the intrinsic modules
  (`iso_fortran_env`, `iso_c_binding`, the IEEE modules) grey.
- **Edges.** An arrow is a use, an extension (from a type to its parent) or a call. A label on it is the cpp condition
  it holds under, which a `use` statement or a `CALL` statement gives; a function reference is never labelled, as the
  parser keeps no condition for it. A dotted arrow goes from a generic to its specific procedures, and one labelled
  "submodule of" from a submodule to its parent.
- **Links.** A node links to the heading that documents it; a routine from outside the project links to the interface
  body that documents it, if there is one.

The Mermaid holds fixed mid tones for each kind of node, which read on light and dark backgrounds whatever draws
them. With Pele, `fumadocs-fortran/style.css` draws the nodes in the theme's colours instead, and the lines, arrows
and labels in Fumadocs' (see [Components](/docs/reference/components#graphs)).

## What the model leaves out

The parser reads statements, not a compiler's semantics, and keeps what the pages need. So:

- **Local declarations.** The model keeps the dummy arguments and results of procedures, the components of types
  and the variables of modules, and not the variables a procedure or a program declares for itself. A call through a
  local variable's type-bound procedure is not drawn, and a call through a procedure pointer the caller declares gets
  no card and no edge.
- **Function references in "Calls:" lines.** The line lists `CALL` statements only. Function references show in the
  call graphs, where the model can place them.
- **Intrinsic procedures in graphs**, which draw none but the coarray collectives. In a "Calls:" line, an intrinsic
  subroutine such as `random_number` has its card.
- **Protected access.** `protected` is an attribute of a variable, shown as its pill, and no access level: the model
  has `public` and `private` only, so `protected` in `--display` matches no entity.
- **COMMON blocks and NAMELIST groups**, which the model holds, with their members and lines, and no page shows, nor
  the common blocks a block data unit gives initial values to.
- **ENTRY, DATA and EQUIVALENCE statements**, which the parser skips: an ENTRY point has no entry of its own, and the
  values of a DATA statement are not shown, though an initial value in a declaration is.
- **Vendor directives** such as `!dir$`, `!dec$` and `!gcc$`, which the model keeps and no page shows.
- **Branches switched off** by cpp, which are not read, and fypp sources, which are skipped.

How the pages compare with FORD's and Doxygen's, and how their settings carry over, is in
[Move from FORD or Doxygen](/docs/guides/from-ford-doxygen).
